Digital modes and linked systems

Digital voice and linked nodes

DMR: verify frequency, color code, time slot, talkgroup and contact type. D-STAR, System Fusion, P25 and other systems require their own mode and routing settings.

AllStarLink and EchoLink are network services, not RF modes. Their paths may depend on local power, a working node and internet.

Before linking, listen and identify. Check that the destination permits the connection. Use only documented commands for that particular node.

Prevent a stuck PTT or feedback loop. Test the local RF simplex path as an independent backup. Do not assume stored node numbers remain current.

FT8 and FT4 setup

Use the correct mode and frequency list in your installed software. Most HF operation uses USB or USB data even below 10 MHz.

Confirm call sign, grid, computer clock, audio input and output, CAT control and PTT. Aim for clock error well below one second.

Turn off speech processing. Set audio drive conservatively and follow the radio manual for ALC. Watch temperature and duty cycle.

FT8 alternates 15-second periods; FT4 uses 7.5-second periods. Verify the selected transmit period and audio offset. Keep Enable Tx off until ready.

Winlink in the field

Prepare the account, software, radio interface and a tested gateway or peer before departure. Download needed forms and channel information for offline use.

Gateway service often uses internet beyond the RF hop. Radio-only and peer-to-peer need their own compatible configuration; an ordinary internet email address is not a direct RF destination.

Choose the correct message type, connection mode and recipient. Keep messages short. Send a test and verify delivery with the recipient.

An outbox clearing or gateway acceptance is not recipient acknowledgment. Follow up on critical messages.

References: Winlink

APRS and position reporting

144.390 MHz FM is the customary terrestrial APRS frequency in North America. Confirm the local plan, path and mode before use.

Set the correct call sign and SSID, position, symbol, comment and sensible beacon rate. Avoid aggressive digipeater paths.

A transmitted packet is not proof it was received. A map report on the internet is not proof a field station can hear you.

For messages, request and verify acknowledgment where supported. State coordinate format and datum when passing a position by voice.

References: APRS

Mesh and satellite backups

Mesh: match hardware band, region, modem preset, channel and permitted security settings. Meshtastic, MeshCore and Reticulum are not automatically interoperable.

Test the whole message path and acknowledgment. A paired phone or visible neighbor does not prove destination reachability.

Part 15 operation and amateur licensed operation have different requirements. Do not mix amateur power privileges with encrypted amateur messages.

Satellite: verify current spacecraft status, uplink, downlink, mode, pass and Doppler plan. Hear the downlink first; use minimum necessary power. Never transmit blindly at a remembered satellite frequency.

References: FCC rules; Mesh settings; Mesh encryption; Satellite status
