Q signals and net codes

Q signals one

A question mark turns a Q signal into a question. These are common amateur meanings; use plain language for public-service voice traffic.

Code Statement or request
QRL I am busy; frequency in use
QRM Interference from signals
QRN Atmospheric noise or static
QRO Increase transmitter power
QRP Decrease transmitter power
QRQ Send faster
QRS Send more slowly
QRT Stop transmitting

References: Q signals; Operating aids

Q signals two

Code Statement or request
QRV I am ready
QRX Wait; I will call again
QRZ Who is calling me
QSB Your signal is fading
QSL I acknowledge receipt
QSO Communicate directly
QSY Change frequency
QTH My location is
QRG Your exact frequency is
QSK I can hear between signals

QSL confirms receipt; it does not guarantee a request was carried out. QRP also commonly describes low-power amateur operation.

References: Q signals; Operating aids

Q signals for traffic

Code Statement or request
QRU I have nothing for you
QTC I have messages for you
QSP I will relay
QST General call to amateurs
QTR The time is
QSA Your signal strength is
QSD Your keying is defective

QST is customary amateur usage. Ask for fills in plain language whenever a code could be misunderstood.

References: Q signals; Operating aids; Message procedure

Traffic net QN signals

QN signals below are specialized amateur traffic-net procedure, not the international Q-code series. Follow your net control.

Code Net meaning
QNI Report in or check in
QNU Traffic for you; stand by
QNX You are excused from net
QNF Net is free or undirected
QNC Traffic for all net stations
QND Net is directed
QNE Entire net stand by

References: Message procedure
